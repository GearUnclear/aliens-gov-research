# Expert perspectives

## Avi Loeb — *What Will Be the Content of the Website Aliens.gov?* (Medium, March 2026)

**Who:** Baird Professor of Science at Harvard, founding director of Harvard's Black Hole
Initiative, former chair of Harvard's astronomy department (2011–2020), founder of the Galileo
Project, former PCAST member.

**Argument:** Loeb thinks U.S. Government sensors monitoring Earth for national security are
plausibly the first systems that would detect non-human technology, so the question of *what*
ends up on aliens.gov is genuinely consequential — not merely political theater.

**Best-case scenario (his words):** The site features *"high-resolution images or videos that
show objects whose dynamics or appearance indicate without any doubt non-human made
technologies."*

**Worst-case scenario:** Inconclusive data, heavily redacted documents, or material insufficient
to draw any meaningful conclusion — an outcome he argues would *fuel* conspiracy theories rather
than dispel them.

**Stakes Loeb cites:** aviation safety, national security, and — if any UAP turns out to be
non-human — what he calls *"the next Copernican revolution"* in humanity's understanding of its
cosmic position.

**What Loeb does *not* claim:** he names no specific UAP programs, no specific incidents, and no
inside source on what aliens.gov will actually publish. The piece is explicitly speculative.

## AARO insiders (via DefenseScoop reporting)

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, the DoD body that has examined the largest publicly
known volume of UAP cases, has consistently maintained on the record that **none** of its >2,000
investigated cases has produced evidence of extraterrestrial technology. AARO's existence and
public stance are an important counterweight to the implicit framing of "release the files →
revelation."

## Senate aide (via UFONews.co)

A Senate aide quoted in UFONews argued Trump's UFO disclosure order **does nothing new** beyond
existing statutory obligations (the 2023 NDAA UAP records-release framework, ongoing National
Archives rolling publications). Under this view, aliens.gov could end up being a re-skinned
front-end on disclosures that were already required by law.

## Mainstream science press (Scientific American)

Scientific American's coverage frames Trump's order as **obscuring** the scientific search for
extraterrestrial life — arguing that conflating UAP records (mostly mundane sensor artifacts and
classified terrestrial tech) with the SETI/biosignature scientific program does a disservice to
both.

## Update, 2026-08-23 — how the March predictions aged

### Avi Loeb — from outside speculator to inside chair

The author of *"What Will Be the Content of the Website Aliens.gov?"* now works for the answer's
custodian: on **2026-06-17** Loeb was named chair of the federal **UAP Science Advisory
Council**, reporting to the new interagency UAP Governance Board. Three ironies:

1. **His question had a null answer.** aliens.gov's content turned out to be an immigration
   dashboard; the records went to war.gov/UFO.
2. **His worst-case scenario is roughly his own current assessment.** Reviewing the PURSUE
   releases (NewsNation, June 2026), Loeb says nothing released so far convinces him of
   non-human origin — distances unmeasured, key details redacted. His July Medium essay title
   is the thesis: *"A Distance Measurement is Worth a Thousand Words."*
3. **The council embodies the access problem he warned about.** It is unpaid, unbudgeted, and
   has no classified access — its scientists see only pre-filtered, already-public material
   (Harvard Crimson, 2026-07-11). Loeb continues publishing his separate 3I/ATLAS anomaly
   catalog on Medium throughout.

### AARO — position unchanged after five releases

The FY2025 annual report (~2026-07-21) and every PURSUE tranche keep AARO's line intact: **no
verifiable evidence of extraterrestrial technology or beings**; ~40% of cases unresolved for
lack of data, not for anomaly. Director Kosloski's June report describing an orange "mother
orb" releasing smaller orbs shows the office publishing stranger material without changing its
bottom line.

### The Senate aide's "nothing new" prediction — half wrong

The February claim that Trump's directive added nothing beyond statute undersold PURSUE: five
tranches, an NDA-waiver memo, and two new oversight bodies are more than the rolling NARA
publications required. But the aide's deeper point survives — none of it has disclosed anything
AARO hadn't already characterized, and the UAP Disclosure Act (the change with legal teeth)
still isn't law.

### The skeptics — engaged, not vindicated or refuted

- **Michael Shermer** joined Loeb's council (while maintaining a standing $1,000 bet that alien
  disclosure will never happen), then publicly apologized to Grusch and deleted old tweets — the
  skeptic/debunker line becoming a live institutional question.
- **Mick West** fact-checks individual PURSUE cases on Metabunk; **Steven Novella** ("Another
  Pentagon UAP Release – More Dots," 2026-08-11) argues the drops add dots without connecting
  them.
- From the *opposite* flank, disclosure activists (Steven Greer, Ron James) call the
  panel-and-tranche process a controlled dead end — process skepticism rather than phenomenon
  skepticism.

### The whistleblower track

**David Grusch** (now Special Advisor to Burlison's task force) escalated in June: alleged
billions in annual off-books DIA "slush fund" UAP spending, filed an IG complaint. **Luis
Elizondo** toured 18 states, disclosed an alleged elimination-plot warning, and timed his
second book (*Reckoning*) for 2026-08-27. The 2026-07-31 ODNI memo gives their successors a
sanctioned channel — to AARO/PURSUE, not to the public.

## Where the perspectives converge

Even Loeb (the most disclosure-optimistic) and the Senate-aide / SciAm camp (most skeptical)
converge on one practical point: **the value of aliens.gov depends entirely on what gets posted,
not on the existence of the domain itself.** Until content lands, the registration is a
content-free signal.

*(2026-08-23: content landed, and the convergence held in an unexpected way — what got posted
made aliens.gov worthless to every camp in the UAP debate simultaneously. The disclosure
argument moved wholesale to war.gov/UFO, where the same convergence test now applies tranche by
tranche.)*
